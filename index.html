PDF-Omni: Poincaré Dual Disk Distortion Field-based Recurrent Update for Omnidirectional Stereo Matching

ECCV 2026

Yunseok Yang1, Eunjin Son1, Sang Jun Lee1
1Jeonbuk National University
Paper arXiv Video Code

PDF-Omni estimates 360° metric depth from four fisheye cameras — each single frame reconstructs the entire surrounding scene as a colored point cloud: road, lane markings, vehicles, pedestrians, and building facades.

Abstract

Omnidirectional stereo matching (OSM) estimates 360° depth from multi-view fisheye images, where inherent fisheye distortion causes sparse matching cues in seam regions. However, existing methods rely on spatially uniform update policies, degrading performance in these challenging regions.

To address this limitation, we propose PDF-Omni, a geometry-aware OSM framework built on a Poincaré Dual Disk (PDD) distortion field that models spatially varying geometric uncertainty across seam regions. We introduce a distortion-informed GRU (DiGRU) with distortion-aware attention (DAA) to selectively expand the receptive field in high-uncertainty regions while preserving efficiency in low-uncertainty ones. We further present a local entropy cumulative (LEC) loss to supervise high-entropy cost distributions in ambiguous regions.

Experimental results demonstrate state-of-the-art performance on five datasets, with consistent gains especially in seam regions — while running 41.9% faster with 15.9% less memory than OmniMVS+.

Why Seam Regions?

Depth errors of existing omnidirectional stereo methods concentrate at seam regions (azimuth θ=±π/2), where fisheye radial distortion is most severe and neither the front nor the back camera provides a dominant viewpoint. Azimuth-wise MAE (c) shows error peaks at seam regions across all prior methods; PDF-Omni flattens these peaks.

Method

Overview of PDF-Omni. A shared 2D CNN with multi-resolution transformers extracts multi-view features from four fisheye images. Spherical sweeping constructs reference and target volumes, from which an encoding volume and a correlation volume are derived; entropy-regularized optimal transport with soft-argmax produces the initial depth. DAA converts the PDD distortion field into a spatial attention map, and DiGRU iteratively refines the prediction through distortion-modulated recurrent updates.

Poincaré Dual Disk Distortion Field

The stereographic projection from the hyperboloid model to the Poincaré disk is structurally analogous to the projection from the hemisphere to the fisheye image plane: both share a boundary-divergent metric that grows nonlinearly toward the edge. We instantiate two Poincaré disks at the front and back camera centers; their geodesic distances are fused into a single scalar field that unifies radial distortion magnitude and view-transition ambiguity, peaking exactly at seam regions. The field is precomputed once per resolution and reused across all refinement iterations.

Distortion-Informed Iterative Refinement

DAA injects the PDD distortion field into a CBAM-style attention block, producing a spatial attention map driven by geometric uncertainty rather than appearance alone. DiGRU maintains two GRU branches — a compact 1×1 branch for low-distortion regions and a separable large-kernel (1×7 + 7×1) branch for seam regions — and blends them per pixel with the attention map, selectively enlarging the receptive field only where geometric ambiguity demands it.

Local Entropy Cumulative Loss

Instead of supervising only the regressed depth value, the LEC loss regularizes the depth probability volume itself: each pixel's supervision radius adapts to the entropy of its cost distribution, relaxing supervision where correspondences are inherently ambiguous. A stop-gradient on the radius prevents the network from inflating entropy to widen its own tolerance, reducing gradient noise from ambiguous seam-region correspondences.

Qualitative Results

Comparison of fine-tuned models on OmniThings, OmniHouse, Sunny, and real-scene data. PDF-Omni produces sharper depth boundaries and fewer artifacts in seam regions (dashed boxes) than MDP-Omni and RomniStereo.

Quantitative Results

PDF-Omni achieves state-of-the-art results on all five OSM benchmarks on every metric after fine-tuning, and the largest gains appear exactly where the method is aimed: on OmniHouse the >1 error drops by 49% relative to the best prior method (2.86 vs. 5.61), and on OmniThings from 29.53 to 17.75. Efficiency & Generalizability

On an RTX 3090, PDF-Omni runs at 0.36 s/frame with 5,284 MB of GPU memory — 41.9% faster and 15.9% lighter than OmniMVS+32 (0.62 s, 6,284 MB) while being substantially more accurate.

DAA+DiGRU also works as a drop-in replacement for the recurrent unit of existing RAFT-based OSM frameworks: plugged into RomniStereo32, it reduces >1 error on OmniThings by 15.4% and MAE on OmniHouse by 21.3%, outperforming the Selective-Stereo SRU alternative across all datasets.
